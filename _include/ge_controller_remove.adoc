Remove the impaired controller from the chassis, note its MAC address and confirm you have the correct replacement controller part number.

.Steps
//. Wait for any data in cache memory to be written to the drives.

//The green Cache Active LED (NV Caching Active LED) on the back of each controller is on when cached data needs to be written to the drives. You must wait for this LED to turn off.

. On the impaired controller, make sure the NV Caching Active (green) LED is off.
+
When the NV Caching Active (green) LED is off, any data in cache memory has been written to the drives and it is safe to remove the impaired controller.
+
NOTE: If the NV Caching Active (green) LED is on, cached data is being written to the drives. You must wait for the process to complete and the NV Caching Active LED to turn off. However, if the LED remains on for longer than five minutes, contact https://mysupport.netapp.com/site/global/dashboard[NetApp Support] before continuing with this procedure.
+
The NV Caching Active (green) LED is located next to the NV icon on the controller.
+
image::../media/drw_g_nvmem_led_ieops-1839.svg[NV status LED location]
+
[cols="1,4"]

NV icon and NV Caching Active LED on the controller

[start=2]
. Put on an ESD wristband or take other antistatic precautions.

. Label each cable that is attached to the impaired controller.

. Disconnect the power on the impaired controller:
+
NOTE: The power supply (PSU) does not have a power switch.
+
.. Open the power cord retainer.
.. Unplug the power cord from the PSU and set it aside.

+
. Unplug all cables from the impaired controller.

. Remove the impaired controller:
+
The following illustration shows the operation of the controller handles (from the left side of the controller) when removing a controller:
+
image::../media/drw_g_and_t_handles_remove_ieops-1837.svg[controller handle operation to remove a controller]
+
[cols="1,4"]

|===
a|
image::../media/icon_round_1.png[Callout number 1]
a|
On both ends of the controller, push the vertical locking tabs outward to release the handles to their horizontal position.
a|
image::../media/icon_round_2.png[Callout number 2] 
a|
* Pull the handles towards you to unseat the controller from the midplane.
+
As you pull, the handles extend out from the controller and then you feel some resistance, keep pulling.
+
* Slide the controller out of the chassis while supporting the bottom of the controller, and place it on a flat, static-free work surface.
a|
image::../media/icon_round_3.png[Callout number 3] 
a|
If needed, rotate the handles upright (next to the tabs) to move them out of the way.
|===

. Make note of the MAC address on the side of the impaired controller.
+
You use the MAC address later in the controller replacement procedure.

. Confirm that the part number on the impaired controller is the same as the impaired controller's replacement part number in SANtricity System Manager:
+
CAUTION: *Possible loss of data access*: If the two part numbers are not the same, do not attempt this procedure. The presence of mismatched controllers causes the replacement controller to lock down when you bring it online.
+
.. Make note of the part number on the side of the impaired controller.

.. Locate the impaired controller's replacement part number displayed in the details area of the Recovery Guru. 
+
If you need to find this number manually, follow these steps:
+
... Select *Hardware* > *Controllers and components*.
... Click the controller graphic for your impaired controller (Controller A or Controller B) to view the context menu.
... Click *View settings*.
... On the *Base* tab, make a note of the *Replacement Part Number* for the controller.

.. Compare the two part numbers to confirm they are the same.

.. The next step depends on whether the part numbers are the same or not:
+
[options="header" cols="1,2"]

|===

The two part numbers are the same

Continue to the next step.
a|

a|
You cannot continue with this procedure. 

. Contact https://mysupport.netapp.com/site/global/dashboard[NetApp Support] for assistance.

. Reinstall the impaired controller into the chassis (within 60 minutes of its removal) to prevent overheating of the storage system.
